# Chronic Absenteeism: Early Signals That Matter

**A Stakeholder Brief for Oakland Unified School District**

*Analysis of U.S. Department of Education data (2020-23) and OUSD student records (2017-24)*

---

## The Big Picture

Chronic absenteeism --- missing 10% or more of school days --- is a nationwide problem. In 2022-23, **1 in 4 U.S. students** (13.4 million) were chronically absent. OUSD's challenge is significantly more acute: our rate peaked at **56.3%** that same year before declining to **32.1%** in 2023-24.

![National vs. California vs. OUSD Trend](figures/fig1_national_ca_ousd_trend.png)
*OUSD's post-pandemic spike far exceeded state and national averages, but is now recovering.*

---

## Who Is Most Affected?

Both national data and OUSD records point to the same groups at elevated risk.

### By income

Economically disadvantaged students make up **67.5% of all chronically absent students nationally**. In OUSD, SED students are **2.5x more likely** to be chronically absent than non-SED peers (39.2% vs. 15.7%).

![OUSD SED and SpEd Rates](figures/fig8_ousd_sed_sped.png)

### By race/ethnicity

Disparities are consistent at both the national and local level. In OUSD:

| Group              | Chronic Rate |
|--------------------|:------------:|
| Pacific Islander   | 55.1%        |
| African American   | 43.5%        |
| Native American    | 40.4%        |
| Latino             | 34.9%        |
| OUSD Average       | 32.2%        |
| White              | 17.5%        |
| Asian              | 16.3%        |

![OUSD Chronic Rate by Ethnicity](figures/fig7_ousd_ethnicity.png)

### By grade level

Chronic absence follows a U-shaped curve: elevated in Pre-K/K, lowest in elementary, then **escalating sharply from 9th grade onward**. By 12th grade, more than half of OUSD students are chronically absent.

![OUSD Grade Level Rates](figures/fig9_ousd_grade_level.png)
*The 9th-grade transition is a critical intervention point --- chronic rates jump 5.5 percentage points from 8th grade.*

---

## The Strongest Early Warning Signs

We analyzed 113,000+ year-to-year student transitions to find which prior-year indicators best predict future chronic absence. Three stand out:

### 1. Prior-year attendance rate

This is the **single most powerful predictor**. A student's attendance last year tells us more about their risk this year than any other factor.

| Last Year's Attendance | This Year's Chronic Rate |
|------------------------|:------------------------:|
| Below 80%              | **85.7%**                |
| 80-85%                 | 61.9%                    |
| 85-90%                 | 39.1%                    |
| 90-95%                 | 21.6%                    |
| Above 95%              | 16.6%                    |

![Prior Attendance Signal](figures/fig10_prior_attendance_signal.png)
*Students with attendance below 80% last year have a 5x higher risk than those above 95%.*

### 2. Prior-year GPA

Academic struggles and attendance problems reinforce each other. Students with GPAs below 1.0 have a **77.2% chronic absence rate** the following year.

![Prior GPA Signal](figures/fig12_prior_gpa_signal.png)

### 3. Prior-year suspensions

Any suspension in the prior year nearly **doubles** the likelihood of chronic absence (58.3% vs. 31.8%).

![Prior Suspension Signal](figures/fig13_prior_suspension_signal.png)

## How These Signals Compare

Not all risk factors carry equal weight. Prior-year behavioral signals are far more predictive than demographic characteristics alone.

## What This Means: A Three-Tier Action Framework

### Tier 1 --- Flag before the school year starts

Using data already in our systems, we can identify the highest-risk students each summer:

- **Attendance below 90% last year** --- flag for outreach
- **Attendance below 85% last year** --- assign intensive case management
- **Previously chronic** --- auto-enroll in attendance support
- **GPA below 2.0** --- pair academic and attendance interventions

### Tier 2 --- Prioritize resources for compounding risk

When multiple risk factors overlap, concentrate support:

- **SED students entering 9th grade** with declining attendance
- **Students with disabilities** showing attendance drops
- **Students with prior suspensions** and low GPAs

### Tier 3 --- Target schools in high-need neighborhoods

Chronically absent students live in ZIP codes with **22% more violent crime** on average. Schools in these neighborhoods may benefit from additional support staff and community partnerships.

---

## Key Takeaway

We don't need to wait for students to become chronically absent to know who is at risk. **Prior-year attendance alone identifies the vast majority of future chronically absent students.** Combined with GPA, suspension history, and demographic context, the data we already collect can power an early-warning system that flags at-risk students before the school year begins --- giving us the best chance to intervene early and keep students in school.
